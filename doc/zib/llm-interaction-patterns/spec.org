#+TITLE: llm interaction patterns

Goal: Produce =research.org= with useful information in response to
the ideas presented in this spec.

I want good info on low-tech, low-dependency ways of working with
LLMs.

The first basic thing of course is running an agent like claude code
and prompting it interactively. Another approach is to use =claude -p=
and prompt it as a one-shot.

I am sort of doing that with my =spec.org= thing where I write out
this spec, and then have the LLM do something with it.

Another approach that I've experimented with is the mailing list
approach. That seems pretty intuitive to me - I am sending and
receiving messages, there's a history, quoting, threads, etc.

I'm still in the early stages of this, but just want to get a sense of
what is out there. I would like to develop an effective approach for
working with LLMs.

The email thing appeals to me. It just seems very elixir-y in the
sense of message passing. I can have a natural conversation and then
have it progress into a design doc and then implement it.

See what people are up to these days, and look for parallels to
interaction patterns that predate LLMs. The LLM is of course
interesting technology in its own right, but the thing that is seems
to enable for regular users is lower latency, more parallelized work,
and that has to be something that we've addressed as a software
industry and in other industries.
